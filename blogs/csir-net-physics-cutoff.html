How Much Score Is Required for CSIR NET Physics?

If you are preparing for CSIR NET Physical Sciences, one of the most important questions is: How much score is required to qualify?

The cutoff is not fixed. It changes from one examination cycle to another depending on factors such as paper difficulty, candidate performance and competition.

Important: Do not prepare only for the previous year's cutoff. Use the cutoff as a benchmark and aim for a comfortable margin above it.

What Does the Percentage Mean in Marks?

The Physical Sciences examination has a maximum of 200 marks. Therefore:

Marks = Percentage × 2

For example, a 46.125% cutoff corresponds to approximately 92.25 marks out of 200.

However, candidates should remember that the official cutoff is expressed as a percentage and that the actual examination contains negative marking.

Why Does the CSIR NET Physics Cutoff Change?

1. Difficulty of the paper

A difficult paper can lead to lower overall scores and therefore a lower cutoff.

2. Candidate performance

If a large number of candidates perform strongly, competition for JRF positions increases.

3. Examination cycle

June and December examinations can differ significantly in difficulty and candidate performance.

4. Competition for JRF

The JRF cutoff is generally higher than the Assistant Professor and PhD-only eligibility thresholds.

Minimum Benchmark vs Actual Cutoff

Do not confuse the minimum benchmark with the final subject cutoff.

Minimum benchmark:

33% for General, EWS and OBC categories
25% for SC, ST and PwD categories

The minimum benchmark does not mean that scoring 33% guarantees JRF qualification. The actual Physical Sciences cutoff can be substantially higher.

Is There a Separate Cutoff for Part A, B and C?

No. CSIR-HRDG states that there is no separate cutoff for Parts A, B and C. The cumulative marks from all parts are considered for drawing the merit list.

How Should You Use Previous-Year Cutoffs?

Previous-year cutoff data should be used as a planning tool, not as your final preparation target.

  • Analyse CSIR NET Physics PYQs.
  • Take regular full-length mock tests.
  • Track your score percentage.
  • Identify calculation and conceptual mistakes.
  • Improve accuracy before increasing attempts.
  • Try to maintain a score comfortably above the historical cutoff range.

Final Takeaway

The recent Physical Sciences cutoff demonstrates why it is risky to target one fixed number.

The UR JRF cutoff was 54.188% in December 2024, 51.188% in June 2025 and 46.125% in December 2025.

Therefore, instead of preparing for a particular cutoff, build your preparation around a stronger target.

For JRF aspirants: Aim for approximately 55–60%+ in your full-length mock tests as a preparation benchmark. This is a recommended preparation target, not an official prediction or guarantee of qualification.
